## What you produce with qdaPy is not covered by the AGPL

The material you create by running qdaPy is **your own work**: the figures it
draws, the tables it returns, the coefficients and test results it computes,
and every file you write from them: images, notebooks, reports, manuscripts,
supplementary material, and the analysis scripts you wrote to get there.

None of it is a derivative work of qdaPy, and this License places **no
condition whatsoever** on it. You may keep it private, publish it, license it
under any terms you choose, and sell it. Nothing obliges you to disclose the
source of qdaPy, or anything else, because you used the program to analyse
your data.

This matters here in a way it does not for every library: what comes out of
qdaPy is figures and numbers that go into papers, theses and reports. A
licensing question hanging over a figure in a submitted manuscript is exactly
the kind of doubt nobody needs.

## Where parts of the program end up inside your output

Producing a result sometimes means copying fragments of the program into it:
the axis labels and legend titles of a figure, the wording of a caption such
as *"inertia shown: 42%"*, the colour values of the default palette, the
column names of a returned table, the text of the messages the package emits,
and the structure of a chart specification it renders. For exactly those
fragments you are granted an unlimited, irrevocable permission to use, modify
and distribute them as part of your output, under terms of your own choosing.

That permission covers them **as part of your output**. It is not a licence to
lift program code out of qdaPy and use it as a substitute for the package
itself; for that, the AGPL applies as usual.

## What this does not cover

The reference files shipped in `qdapy/data/`, the exchange contract and its
sample exports, are part of the program, not output you produced. They are
covered by the AGPL like the rest of it. In practice this changes nothing for
anyone analysing their own data.

## Why this document exists

Strictly speaking it changes nothing. The GNU licences have never covered the
output of a program: images made with GIMP belong to the person who drew
them, and the Free Software Foundation says so explicitly in its GPL FAQ. The
belief that a copyleft licence reaches into a program's results is a
persistent myth.

But a myth is enough to make an ethics board, a university legal office or a
publisher hesitate, and qualitative research data is sensitive enough without
a licensing question hanging over it. This clause removes the doubt in
writing.

It only **adds** permission. It takes nothing away from the rights the AGPL
grants you, and it does not make qdaPy any less free.

The same permission applies to zotQDA, to qdaZ and to the R twin qdaR, in the
same words.
